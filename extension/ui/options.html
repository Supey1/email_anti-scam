Welcome! One step to finish

Granblock warns you before you open a scam link, fill in a fake sign-in page, or answer a scam email.

To do that, it needs to look at the websites you visit. Chrome will ask you to allow this. Choose below.

Website access

…Checking…

To stop a scam link before it opens, Granblock looks at the page you're on, the link you click, and the email you open. It does this on this computer: nothing you read or type is sent anywhere.

Protects you from scam ads, fake sign-in pages, and scam emails. Chrome will say the extension can "read and change all your data on all websites."

Only your email in Gmail. Scam websites and ads won't be checked.

Known dangerous websites are still blocked, but nothing else is checked.

You can change this any time, here or in Chrome's extension settings. The choices below only matter for the websites Chrome lets Granblock see.

    Google Safe Browsing

    Safe Browsing is Google's list of fake, harmful and dangerous websites. Turn this on to check the websites you visit, and the links in Gmail emails, against it. It needs your own free Safe Browsing key from Google.

    Off unless you turn it on. Website addresses are not sent. For each page, Granblock sends Google only the first few bytes of scrambled fingerprints of the address, which many unrelated websites share, so Google gets answers without being told which website you opened. Google can still see your computer's address and your key, as any website can. Your key is saved only on this computer.

    What Granblock checks

    • Where a link really goes, compared with what it says. Links made to look like a bank or store, number addresses, and shortened links.
    • Words that scammers use: gift cards, wire transfers, "your account is locked," rushing you, keeping a secret, prizes, delivery fees, and "tech support."
    • The stories scams tell: a grandchild in trouble, a surprise charge with a number to call, guaranteed investment profits or "send crypto, get double back," a job you must pay to start, "we can recover the money you lost" for a fee, and blackmail emails.
    • Pages that ask for a password or card number and send it to a different website.
    • In Gmail, the sender's name compared with the sender's real address, along with the checks above.
    • Advertisements that are clearly marked as ads, when they promise a prize or lead somewhere they should not. Ordinary ads are left alone, and no ad is ever hidden.
    • Websites on a small built-in list of known dangerous addresses. These are stopped before they open.

    Everything is checked on this computer. Nothing you read is sent anywhere, unless you turn on Google Safe Browsing above (then only short fingerprints of addresses are sent, as described there).

    What it cannot check

    • Anything outside Chrome: text messages, phone calls, other programs, and other web browsers.
    • Pages that Chrome does not let extensions look at, such as Chrome's own settings pages, and private (Incognito) windows unless you allow this extension there.
    • Some advertisements: ads inside other websites' frames, and ads that are not marked as ads.
    • A website that sends you somewhere by its own program, a link opened from the right-click menu, or an address typed or pasted into the address bar.
    • Brand-new scams it has never seen.

    "No warning signs found" does not mean something is safe. If you are unsure, do not click. Call someone you trust.

    What Chrome allows Granblock to do, and why

    • Read and change your data on websites (only if you allow it above): to look at the page you're on, the link you click, and the email you open, and show a warning in front of it. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you turn on Google Safe Browsing.
    • Block content on any page: to stop known dangerous websites before they load. Chrome does this matching itself; Granblock doesn't see your browsing history.
    • Storage: to remember these settings and the list of known dangerous websites, on this computer.
    • Scripting: to start or stop checking websites when you change the website access above.

    Granblock never asks for your cookies or your browsing history, and never keeps your emails, the pages you read, or anything you type.

    Your information

    These settings, and any "This is a false alarm" reports you choose to send while testing, are saved only on this computer. A report holds the website's name, what we said, and the time. It never holds what you typed.
