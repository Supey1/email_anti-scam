    What Email Anti-Scam checks

    • Where a link really goes, compared with what it says. Links made to look like a bank or store, number addresses, and shortened links.
    • Words that scammers use: gift cards, wire transfers, "your account is locked," rushing you, keeping a secret, prizes, delivery fees, and "tech support."
    • Pages that ask for a password or card number and send it to a different website.
    • In Gmail, the sender's name compared with the sender's real address, along with the checks above.
    • Advertisements that are clearly marked as ads, when they promise a prize or lead somewhere they should not. Ordinary ads are left alone, and no ad is ever hidden.
    • Websites on a small built-in list of known dangerous addresses. These are stopped before they open.

    Everything is checked on this computer. Nothing you read is sent anywhere.

    What it cannot check

    • Anything outside Chrome: text messages, phone calls, other programs, and other web browsers.
    • Pages that Chrome does not let extensions look at, such as Chrome's own settings pages, and private (Incognito) windows unless you allow this extension there.
    • Some advertisements: ads inside other websites' frames, and ads that are not marked as ads.
    • A website that sends you somewhere by its own program, a link opened from the right-click menu, or an address typed or pasted into the address bar.
    • Brand-new scams it has never seen.

    "No warning signs found" does not mean something is safe. If you are unsure, do not click. Call someone you trust.

    Your information

    These settings, and any "This is a false alarm" reports you choose to send while testing, are saved only on this computer. A report holds the website's name, what we said, and the time. It never holds what you typed.
